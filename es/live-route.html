Live Route

A virtual tour guide that starts at our door on Washington and 14th and takes you to the South Beach worth seeing. Tell it how you are moving, how long you have and what you like — it builds the route and then talks you through it, stop by stop, while you ride.

What is a self-guided tour of South Beach?

Live Route is a free self-guided tour of South Beach from Miami Beach Bikes at 233 14th Street. You choose how you are travelling — on foot, beach cruiser, electric bike, Segway, Trikke or skates — how much time you have, from 30 minutes to half a day, and what you want to see. It then orders the nearest landmarks into a loop, tells you the distance and riding time between each one, and in live mode uses your phone's location to announce each stop as you reach it. No app, no sign-up, no charge.

How it works

A guide in your pocket, not a group to keep up with

Pick your three

Vehicle, time, interests. Six ways to travel, four time budgets, ten things to be into — the combinations land in the hundreds.

We order the stops

It takes the landmarks that match, orders them by what is actually nearest, and only keeps what fits your clock — including the ride back to us.

Ride it live

Live mode uses your phone's location to show the distance and direction to the next stop, and flips over when you arrive.

Or just open Maps

One tap sends the whole loop to Google Maps for turn-by-turn, if you would rather have a voice in your ear.

Every stop on the map

The 23 places Live Route can take you

Written by people who ride past them every day — not scraped from a listings site.

Española Way

The Spanish village · 419 Española Way

Built in 1922 as Whitman's Spanish Colony — a Mediterranean village dropped into Florida. Two blocks of pink stucco, striped awnings and tables in the middle of the street. Al Capone ran a casino upstairs at number 1421.

Open in Maps →

Lincoln Road Mall

The mile-long promenade · Lincoln Rd between Alton & Washington

Morris Lapidus pedestrianised it in 1960 and called it a street for people, not cars. A mile of shops, cafes and street performers. Lock up and walk this one — no wheels on the mall.

Open in Maps →

New World Center

Gehry's concert hall · 500 17th Street

Frank Gehry built it in 2011 with a 7,000 sq ft projection wall on the outside — on Wallcast nights the orchestra plays inside and the park watches it on the wall for free.

Open in Maps →

Miami Beach Botanical Garden

Free green escape · 2000 Convention Center Drive

Two and a half acres of palms, orchids and a Japanese garden, free to walk in. The quietest spot within a mile of Ocean Drive.

Open in Maps →

The Bass Museum

Contemporary art in a 1930 deco shell · 2100 Collins Avenue

Built in 1930 as the Miami Beach Public Library out of keystone — Florida coral rock full of fossils. Look at the walls before you look at the art.

Open in Maps →

Miami Beach Boardwalk

The sea-grape corridor · Boardwalk at 21st Street

Raised walkway through sea grape and palm all the way north. Bikes stay on the paved Beachwalk below — the wooden stretch is for feet only.

Open in Maps →

Beachwalk at 14th

Car-free, ocean on your left · Beachwalk at 14th Street

The paved path that runs the length of the sand. No traffic, no lights, no thinking — just turn right for South Pointe or left for North Beach.

Open in Maps →

Versace Mansion

Villa Casa Casuarina · 1116 Ocean Drive

Built 1930 by Alden Freeman, modelled on the Alcázar de Colón in Santo Domingo. Gianni Versace bought it in 1992 and was shot on these steps in 1997. Today it is a hotel — and the most photographed doorway in Florida.

Open in Maps →

The Clevelander

Ocean Drive's loudest corner · 1020 Ocean Drive

1938, and the pool bar that defines the Ocean Drive soundtrack. Ride past in daylight; come back after dark when the neon is on.

Open in Maps →

Art Deco Welcome Center

Start of the district · 1001 Ocean Drive

Run by the Miami Design Preservation League, the people who saved this district from the bulldozers in the 1970s. Maps, exhibits and the reason any of these buildings still stand.

Open in Maps →

Lummus Park

The candy-coloured lifeguard towers · 10th Street & Ocean Drive

Ten blocks of palm and sand between Ocean Drive and the Atlantic. The lifeguard towers were rebuilt after Hurricane Andrew by architect William Lane, each one a different colour. The 10th Street tower is the one on every postcard.

Open in Maps →

The Wolfsonian

Design, propaganda and industry · 1001 Washington Avenue

A 1927 storage building turned museum of how design shaped the modern world. The lobby fountain alone is worth the stop.

Open in Maps →

The Colony Hotel

The blue neon one · 736 Ocean Drive

1935, by Henry Hohauser. That vertical blue neon sign is the single most recognisable object in Miami Beach — and it is best at dusk, not noon.

Open in Maps →

Ocean Drive & 5th

Where the district begins · 5th Street & Ocean Drive

The southern gate of the Art Deco district. From here to 15th is the largest concentration of Art Deco architecture on earth — about 800 buildings.

Open in Maps →

Joe's Stone Crab

Open since 1913 · 11 Washington Avenue

Older than the city around it. Stone crab season runs mid-October to May, they do not take reservations, and the takeaway window beside the restaurant is the locals' move.

Open in Maps →

South Pointe Park

Where the island ends · 1 Washington Avenue

Seventeen acres at the southern tip, with the 1931 lighthouse and a lawn built for watching cruise ships thread Government Cut. Best light of the day is here, one hour before sunset.

Open in Maps →

South Pointe Pier

Cruise ships at arm's length · South Pointe Pier

Walk out over the jetty and the ships pass close enough to read the names. Sunday afternoons the whole channel is boats.

Open in Maps →

Flamingo Park

Where locals actually go · 1200 Meridian Avenue

Tennis, a track, a pool and shade. Four blocks from Ocean Drive and a completely different city.

Open in Maps →

Sunset Harbour

The neighbourhood side · Purdy Avenue & 18th Street

Where Miami Beach eats when it is not performing: coffee roasters, a fish counter, no neon. Ride here for lunch, not for the view.

Open in Maps →

Belle Isle

First of the Venetian Islands · Venetian Causeway at Belle Isle

Cross the 1926 Venetian Causeway and the whole Downtown skyline opens across Biscayne Bay. Low traffic, flat, and the best golden hour on the island.

Open in Maps →

MacArthur Causeway lookout

Star Island and the port · MacArthur Causeway bike path

Protected path with the cruise terminal on one side and the gates of Star, Palm and Hibiscus Islands on the other. You cannot ride onto Star Island — it is private — but the gate is the photo.

Open in Maps →

Faena District

The gold mammoth · 3201 Collins Avenue

Mid-Beach turned into an arts district by Alan Faena. Damien Hirst's gilded mammoth skeleton stands in a glass case you can see from the street.

Open in Maps →

Fontainebleau

1954, and still showing off · 4441 Collins Avenue

Morris Lapidus's curved masterpiece, where Sinatra filmed and Bond swam. Walk into the lobby — the staircase to nowhere is still there.

Open in Maps →
